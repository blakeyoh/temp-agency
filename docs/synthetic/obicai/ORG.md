# ObiCai Restaurant Group — context

Independent hospitality management, development, and consulting company. Fort Wayne,
Indiana. Formally ObiCai Restaurant Management, Inc.; corporate HQ at 203 East Berry
Street. Both a multi-unit owner-operator of culinary concepts and a B2B advisory
consultancy. Est. 250–300 hospitality staff; roughly $20M in system-wide economic
activity. One of the largest independent hospitality groups in Northeast Indiana.

Origin is a rescue. During the 2008–09 financial crisis, James Khan — an operator with
a Purdue engineering background and years at Biaggi's Ristorante Italiano — was brought
in to turn around a failing North Fort Wayne restaurant, BakerStreet Steakhouse. Local
philanthropists Doug and Patti Wood had bought the business to stop the jobs from
leaving town. Khan restructured the operating model, set guest-centric service
standards, and paid off the acquisition capital. Everything since was built on that.

Dual revenue: direct hospitality and catering (including BakerStreet Catering, a preferred
F&B partner for major regional venues), plus B2B consulting built on 50+ years of
collective executive experience. Three tiers: executive/ownership, shared corporate
services, and unit-level leadership at each restaurant.

## Known strengths
Strong local brand equity and prime downtown real estate — fifteen years at BakerStreet,
nearly a decade downtown, established guest channels and institutional relationships
across Allen County. The portfolio is deliberately diversified so the steakhouse,
gastropub, and Latin concepts hit different dining occasions without cannibalizing each
other. Shared corporate infrastructure centralizes accounting, HR, facilities, and
procurement, which gives them buying power and efficiency standalone restaurants don't
get. They have already shown willingness to adopt operational technology — Panelz digital
displays are deployed across the kitchens to push real-time priorities and menu changes
to the floor.

## Known weaknesses
Decision-making is heavily centralized between the President and the Director of
Operations. Unit-level managers hold end-user influence but no independent budget
approval authority, so anything requiring money stops at two people. In January 2025 the
U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Indiana issued an injunction and entered
civil judgment against the company, its operating concepts, James Khan, and Peter Shuey
jointly and severally in *Julie A. Su v. Obicai Restaurant Management, Inc. et al.* over
Fair Labor Standards Act compliance — pointing to historic weakness in payroll tracking,
overtime calculation, or tip-pooling. Frontline sentiment shows the usual scratch-kitchen
strain: grueling shifts, a ceiling for managers below the executive partner level, and
turnover among line cooks and floor staff. All operations sit in one city, so the revenue
baseline moves with the local economy.

## Opportunities
The FLSA judgment creates real internal pressure for automated timekeeping, digital
compliance auditing, and transparent tip-pooling. Scratch kitchens under wholesale food
inflation make automated inventory tracking, recipe costing, and predictive ordering an
obvious margin lever. Guest data is fragmented across three brands — a unified loyalty
and reservation platform would enable cross-brand marketing and raise guest lifetime
value. Khan's roles with B1 Real Estate Development and the Regional Development
Authority open a path to embed ObiCai's food, beverage, and consulting services into
planned commercial projects across Northeast Indiana.

## Threats
Culinary wage inflation, overtime cost, and volatile protein and dairy pricing compress
margins on scratch menus. The regional labor market for experienced back-of-house staff
is tight enough that sous-chef packages now carry R&D allowances. Competition increasingly
comes from alumni: Johnny Perez, former head chef at Próximo, founded Te Gustó Hospitality
and runs Mercado, Papi's Pizza, and Spoke + Ivy against them. Fine-dining check averages
and corporate banquet bookings both ride on discretionary and corporate spending. And
multi-stakeholder ventures have proven fragile — in early 2024 the group dissolved its
management partnership with The Landing Beer Co. to pull brewing back into its own houses.

## Culture note
A hospitality culture, not a corporate one: the day is a service, and the service either
went well or it didn't. Leadership is owner-operator and hands-on, with an engineer at
the top who thinks in systems and unit economics. Proof is same-day — ticket times, covers,
food cost, a dining room that turned. The organization runs on people who came up through
the floor and the line, and its structural tension is that those people have the clearest
view of what is broken and the least authority to spend money fixing it. It is also, right
now, an organization living under a federal compliance judgment, which changes how anyone
talks about hours, scheduling, and pay.
