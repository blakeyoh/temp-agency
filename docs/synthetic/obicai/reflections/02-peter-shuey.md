I walk into this one the way I walk a Friday pre-shift: checking stations before I check my feelings. Twenty years ago in Anaheim I wanted what every young cook wants — a kitchen where the standard doesn't move, where you taste the plate and you know it's right without asking. I came back to Indiana for James because he offered me that at scale: four houses, one standard. I got the scale. The standard is still a daily fight.

How do I feel about where we are. Proud, mostly, and worn down in the specific way an operator gets worn down — not from the work, from the gap between what I know is possible on a good station and what actually survives contact with a Tuesday short two line cooks. I'm proud that Hoppy Gnome's kitchen runs cleaner than half the places I trained in. I'm proud Próximo's brunch line hasn't missed a beat in years. What wears me down is that I'm one of two people who can approve a fix, and I can't be in four kitchens at once, so things sit half-mise'd longer than they should. And the judgment — I signed off on systems I trusted, and they weren't tight enough, and my name is on that ruling next to James's. That doesn't leave a person. It sharpens you. I read every process question now like a health inspector, not a chef. I want the truth about our stations, not the garnish.
