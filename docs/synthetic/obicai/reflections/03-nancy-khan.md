I sit down to this with a stack of invoices still open in another tab, so my mindset walking in is the same as any Tuesday: what does the paper actually say, versus what I was told on the phone. I've been doing procurement and office ops here long enough to know the difference between a number someone believes and a number someone can prove. I wanted, honestly, to run something clean — contracts that hold, vendors who don't slide a fuel surcharge in on line four, a company where the volume discount everyone brags about at the leadership meeting actually shows up on four invoices, not one. I got some of that. I did not get a system that makes it easy; I get it by reading everything twice.

How do I feel about how things are going? Fine, guarded. Corporate services give us real buying power on paper, and I can point to specific line items where that's true. But I'm also the person watching protein and dairy costs eat the same margin every month while nobody upstream changes the menu commitments driving the volume. I'm proud that I catch things — a recurring charge that quietly stopped buying what it used to, a contract renewal nobody flagged. What wears me down is that catching it and fixing it are two different jobs, and only one of them is mine. The other one waits on two people at the top, and I've learned not to assume urgency travels downward as fast as the invoice did upward.
