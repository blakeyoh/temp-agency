I sit down with this thing on a Tuesday morning after covers from the weekend are already reconciled and the FLSA compliance binder is still open on the credenza. My mindset walking in is engineering, not hospitality — I want to know what the questionnaire is actually measuring before I answer it honestly. Every organization is a system with stocks (cash, trained staff, guest trust, legal standing) and flows (turnover, covers, wage inflation, review backlog), and most people describing "how we operate" are really describing an event, not the structure generating it.

What did I want out of my career? I wanted to build something that outlasted a single good season — I got that. BakerStreet didn't just survive, it became a platform: four concepts, a consulting arm, a real estate play. I got the scale I wanted. I didn't fully get the distributed organization I know, structurally, would be more resilient — I still gatekeep capital and concept approval myself, and I know that's a bottleneck even as I call it decisive.

How do I feel about things now? Proud, and worn down by the same loop. Proud that the shared services infrastructure gives us buying power line cooks at standalone restaurants don't get. Worn down by the judgment — not the dollars, the signal that our payroll and timekeeping systems had a structural gap I should have caught earlier, before the delay between practice and consequence caught up to us in federal court. That's a systems failure sitting on my desk with my name on it, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise today.
