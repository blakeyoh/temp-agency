I walk into this today the way I walk into 6am prep: clear-eyed about what's real and what's decoration. Twenty years ago I wanted to run a kitchen where a dish traveled from Havana or Oaxaca or Lima to a table in Fort Wayne and still told the truth about where it came from — not a costume version of Cuban food for people who've never had it. I got that, mostly. Próximo's menu is genuinely mine, and on a good night I watch a dish survive the whole gauntlet — costed, scaled, held at temp for six hours, fired at 7pm — and still taste like itself. That's the win. That's what I'm proud of.

What wears me down is that I gatekeep authenticity alone. Nobody above me is asking whether the ropa vieja is still ropa vieja after we cut portioning to hit food cost targets — they're asking whether food cost hit target. Protein prices keep climbing, my last sous left with Johnny Perez to open a place two miles away, and I'm running breakfast through late dinner with a brigade that turns over every few months. I have full authority over my line and zero authority over the checkbook that would fix any of it. I've learned not to expect the view from the pass to travel upstairs intact — by the time it does, it's a number on a spreadsheet, stripped of what made it true.

Today I'm answering plainly. Not bitter, not rosy. This is an ordinary week.
