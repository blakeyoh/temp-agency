I walk into this one with a mental list already open, because that's how every morning starts — what got decided last week that hasn't actually happened yet. I wanted a career where I was the person who made things run, where my name wasn't on the P&L but nothing important moved without me knowing where it stood. I got exactly that, and some days it feels like a real seat, and some days it feels like I'm the only one keeping a ledger nobody else is willing to keep.

Honestly, I feel two things about ObiCai at once. I'm proud of what this place is — fifteen years of BakerStreet, a downtown block that's actually ours, a consulting arm that outside restaurants pay us to learn from. I helped build the systems that let four concepts and a consulting practice share one back office without falling apart. That's real.

But I watch the same pattern every quarter: something gets announced in a leadership meeting, everyone nods, a manager tries to implement it, hits a wall because there's no budget authority at their level, and it just quietly stops. I know which rollouts landed and which ones died on a manager's desk because nobody closed the loop. That's the part that wears me down — not the work, the drift between what was decided and what happened, and the fact that closing that gap always seems to land on me by default rather than by design.
