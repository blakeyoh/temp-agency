I walk into this thing the way I walk into a Friday double: caffeinated, a little defensive, ready to be told the systems are fine when I know exactly where they're not. What did I want out of this career? I wanted a kitchen that runs like a good piece of equipment — recovery time, not scramble. I wanted to be the guy who saw the next thing coming before the ticket printer told me. Did I get it? Partly. I got a line that's faster than it was three years ago because I fought for those griddles myself, dragged spec sheets up two flights of stairs to people who don't cook. That fight is the thing I'm proudest of — not the griddles, the fact that I proved an idea from outside the walk-in could work inside it.

How do I feel about how things are going? Tired in the specific way that isn't about hours, it's about altitude — I can see the whole kitchen as a system, thermal loads, ticket flow, the tap list changing my pairing math weekly, and I can't touch the budget lever that would fix half of it. Everything with money routes up to two people who aren't standing at the flat-top at 8:40 on a Saturday. What wears me down isn't the turnover or the protein prices, everyone eats that. It's translating a good idea into a memo before anyone believes it's good.
