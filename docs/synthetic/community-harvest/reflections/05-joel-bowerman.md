I walk into this questionnaire the way I walk into a route audit — half data, half suspicion that the map is lying to me. Fourteen years ago I thought I wanted to run something big, a regional operation with real systems, real logistics, real numbers. I got that. Nine counties, seven programs, three hundred agencies I have to actually know by name and by loading dock. What I didn't fully bargain for is how much of the job is translation work — taking a program that was designed, in form, for a Fort Wayne parking lot and asking whether it's even the same thing once it lands in a LaGrange township with no cell signal and no bus. Some days I think we've gotten that right. The Farm Wagon model traveled. Other days I watch an agency running Community Cupboard off a paper sign-in sheet next to our Macola dashboard and I wonder whether we've quietly let the "choice pantry" become something else entirely out there — same name, different content.

I'm proud of the fleet, the produce center, the fact that we haven't let the rural counties become an afterthought the way a lot of regional banks do. What wears me down is the org's instinct to treat every rural adaptation as a diminished version of the "real" downtown program rather than as its own legitimate expression of the same core. We default to exporting the HQ form and calling the gap a training problem. I walk in today skeptical of our own confidence, more curious about what the agencies already know than about what corporate wants to teach them.
