I walk into this thing with a full coffee and a low-grade tiredness that never fully clears, because the fleet doesn't take weekends off and neither do I, not really. What I wanted out of this career was to run something that mattered without being invisible — I got half of that. Nine counties depend on trucks I schedule, and when Farm Wagon shows up in a county with no grocery store, that's real. I'm proud of that. But nobody upstream sees the fleet as a system, they see it as a cost center that occasionally embarrasses them with a breakdown. I got the "matters" part. I didn't get the "someone finally listens to the mechanic before the transmission dies" part.

What wears me down is watching the same failure loop every single season and knowing exactly which decision three levels up causes it, and not having a lever to pull. Fuel spikes, so somebody defers maintenance to hit budget, so a truck goes down in February in Steuben County, so a route gets doubled up, so drivers hit their hours limit, so a delivery slips a week, so a distribution site cancels a pantry day, so a family doesn't eat that Tuesday. Then we do a postmortem on "the truck" instead of the budget cycle that starves preventive maintenance every Q4. I've said this in three different meetings with three different words and it gets nodded at and forgotten by spring.

I feel okay about this place overall — mission's real, people work hard, cold chain is genuinely first-rate. But the org treats every recurring problem as a new surprise instead of a pattern, and that's the thing that actually exhausts me, more than the driving or the diesel bills ever could.
