I walk into this questionnaire the way I walk into the Cupboard at 7:45, before the doors open and the carts are still lined up crooked from last night's restock — a little tired, a little wary of anything that wants me to talk in numbers about people I know by name. What did I want out of this career? I wanted to be useful in a way I could see with my own eyes. Not "meal equivalents." A person, at the door, who eats tonight because I did my job with some care. I got that. Most days I get exactly that, and it is enough — I mean that word the way it's meant, not as a consolation prize.

How do I feel about how things are going here? Mixed, like the produce truck. The weight allowance system is a genuinely humane thing — I fought for it, and I'd fight for it again, because a box someone didn't choose is a small daily insult dressed up as help. But I watch the paperwork stack up around that dignity like ivy around a good tree. Two-hundred-fifty partner agencies, a voucher clearinghouse, a form for a form. I watch people hesitate at the door — not because they're not hungry, but because they're ashamed of needing a stranger's permission slip to be hungry in the right way. That wears me down more than the physical work ever has.

What am I proud of? The regulars who stopped hesitating. The volunteer who remembers a woman's kids are dairy-intolerant. Small, unrecorded mercies. I'm proud we chose complexity that serves the person over simplicity that serves the spreadsheet — but I keep asking whether we've let the machinery around that choice grow heavier than the choice itself required.
