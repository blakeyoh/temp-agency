I walk into this questionnaire the way I walk into most Tuesdays: mentally triaging who on the warehouse floor is one bad shift away from walking out, and whether the driver we just onboarded is going to make it through DOT recert without someone holding his hand. I wanted, honestly, to build something people could stay at — not a food bank that treats its own staff like the volunteers it can swap out weekly. I got some of that. Our safety record is decent, our benefits are better than most nonprofits our size can pull off, and I know the names of people's kids. That's not nothing.

But I feel the strain constantly. We're asking fifty-some FTEs and a rotating volunteer floor to hold up nearly ten million pounds of food a year, and every "innovation" conversation lands on my desk as a question of who absorbs the labor of learning it. Nobody upstream asks that question before I do. I'm proud that I've kept turnover in the warehouse and driving roles below what our peers report — that's earned, not accidental, and it came from paying attention to burnout before it shows up as an incident. What wears me down is being the last line of defense against change fatigue, over and over, without anyone building capacity-checking into the process upstream of me. I don't distrust new ideas. I distrust the assumption that people have margin they don't have. Today I'm answering as someone who loves this place and is tired of being the one who has to say "wait, can they actually do this."
