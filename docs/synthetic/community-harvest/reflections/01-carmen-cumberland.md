I walk into this questionnaire the way I walk into a Single Audit: expecting to find something, hoping it's small. Twenty-five years at this food bank, four before that in Navy uniform, and I still count everything twice before I sign it. That's not a personality quirk, that's the job — I'm the last signature on this organization, and nobody downstream gets to be surprised by what I approve.

What did I want out of my career? I wanted to be useful in a way that showed up on a ledger — meals moved, dollars accounted for, an audit with no findings. I got that, more than I expected when I was doing books for Catholic Charities in '98. I built the finance and HR function here from nothing, ran it dual-hatted for over a decade, and now I run the whole operation with a balance sheet I'd stack against anyone in this sector. That's mine. Nobody handed it to me.

How do I feel about how things are going? Steady, stretched, and a little exposed. Revenue's up ten percent but so is need, and our labor force is more than half volunteer — that's not slack, that's a supply line that can evaporate on a bad-weather Saturday. I'm proud of the cold chain, the blast-freeze line, the fleet reaching nine counties. What wears me down is watching 300 agencies run on spreadsheets and paper while HQ runs on Macola, knowing any tech pitch that isn't provably self-funding gets a no from me — and knowing that's the right call even when it slows us down.
