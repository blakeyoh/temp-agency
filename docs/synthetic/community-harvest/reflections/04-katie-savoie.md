I walk into this one thinking about the gala P&L and the Salon Endowment gap in the same breath, which is basically my whole job. What did I want out of my career? I wanted to be the person in the room who could turn a soft ask — "we need help" — into a hard, fundable case that a Kroger regional VP or a program officer at a private foundation would actually sign off on. I got that. I run the largest revenue engine outside government commodities here, and I built the Kroger and Walmart relationships from cold intros into recurring six-figure commitments. That's real.

But I feel the tension every day between what donors and corporate partners will fund — a shiny new mobile pantry, a matching gift campaign, a capacity grant with a nice logo attached — and what the org actually needs, which is unglamorous plumbing: shared systems across 300 agencies, route optimization, data that isn't stuck in somebody's spreadsheet. I've learned to translate ops pain into grant-fundable capacity asks, and that's my proudest trick. What wears me down is how often I'm the only one thinking about the "after the check clears" part — the activation, the reporting burden I've promised a funder, the partner relationship I have to keep healthy for the next ask. Leadership treats a signed grant as the finish line. I treat it as the opening of a new account I now have to manage forever.
