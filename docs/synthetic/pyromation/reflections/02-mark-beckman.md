I walk into this one the way I walk into a month-end close: expecting to defend numbers to somebody who wasn't in the room when they were made. Since November 2022 that's Norris and TASI corporate, and I'm the one who has to make Fort Wayne's story survive their scrutiny. That's not a complaint — it's the job I signed up for when I came from Cooper Standard, where you learned fast that a plant controller's opinion means nothing without a documented variance behind it.

What did I want out of a career that started with a BS from Manchester and a few years of postgrad business coursework at Indiana Wesleyan? Rigor that gets respected. I got it. I own the COGS models on the hardest costing problem in this business — high-mix, low-volume, engineered-to-order — and I've built the discipline to price platinum, nickel, and chromium exposure into decisions before it bites us. That I'm proud of.

How do I feel about how things are going? Mixed, honestly. The two-tier approval gate is correct — it should be hard to spend money on an unproven idea — but I watch good shop-floor initiatives die in review because nobody wrote the return down, and I watch morale erode as quotas replace the old family-run trust. What wears me down is refereeing between a sixty-year engineering culture and a corporate parent that doesn't yet know this plant's rhythm. I call it straight either way. That's the job.
