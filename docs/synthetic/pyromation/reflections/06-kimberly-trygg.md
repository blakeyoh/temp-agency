I sit down with this thing after a morning that started with a machinist asking me, again, whether the new PTO accrual is "TASI's way of clawing back what Pete gave us." I didn't have a clean answer, and he clocked that. That's my mindset today: I notice first who wasn't in the room when a decision got made, and I notice second whether the people who have to live with it were told the truth about why. Fifteen years in Midwest manufacturing HR taught me the mechanics; nearly four years here have taught me that mechanics don't matter if the floor stops believing you.

I wanted, honestly, to run HR somewhere I could build the whole system — not patch a legacy one mid-acquisition. I got something harder and probably better: I got to be the person translating two cultures to each other in real time, with recruiting numbers that don't forgive a bad quarter. Did I get what I wanted? Partly. I have the seat. I don't always have the say.

I'm proud that people still come to me first, before HR becomes a formality. That trust is fragile and I protect it like inventory. What wears me down is watching good tradespeople — welders, machinists we can't replace in this labor market — go quiet instead of raising concerns, because quiet is what surviving an acquisition taught them. Silence isn't compliance. It's data. I just don't always get it upstairs in time.
