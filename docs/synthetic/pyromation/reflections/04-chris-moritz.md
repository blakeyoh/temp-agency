I walk into this one the way I walk into any assessment — looking for where the paper trail is thin. That's not cynicism, it's training. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen, and I've built my whole career on making sure the things that matter here are logged: revisions, calibrations, non-conformances, corrective actions. I got what I wanted out of this career, mostly. I built the CAD-to-CNC pipeline myself, from nothing, and I run an NVLAP lab that outside auditors can't poke a hole in. That's real. Nobody handed me that.

How do I feel about where things are going? Mixed, and I'll be honest about that here since nobody's grading it. The TASI acquisition brought real discipline — reporting structure, two-tier approval, corporate rigor I actually respect on the quality side. But I watch good machinists and CNC programmers leave over quotas and comp, and I watch initiatives die at the new approval gate not because they're bad ideas but because nobody wrote them up properly. I'm proud that everything I personally own is auditable by a stranger with no context. What wears me down is watching how much of this place still runs on institutional memory instead of documented procedure outside my own domain — and knowing that the gap between "we know how to do this" and "we can prove we do this consistently" is exactly where audits, and companies, get hurt.
