I walk into this one the way I walk into a Monday production review: coffee, the four-day board up on the screen, and a mental list of what could knock us off tempo this week. Nineteen years in, I don't get nervous about surveys, but I do get particular about them — I've seen enough consultant instruments dressed up as strategy to know most of them measure how well you fill in a box, not how the plant actually runs. I want out of my career what I got, mostly: I wanted to own something real, machine to shipment, and I do. Manufacturing Engineer to GM to Senior Managing Director is the ladder I set for myself back when I was still lining up thermocouple sheaths, and I climbed the whole thing without leaving the building. What I didn't fully bargain for is the two-tier gate since November '22 — I built my career on being the guy who could say yes on the floor, and now every serious capital ask needs a second signature that isn't mine. I'm proud of the four-day guarantee; that's the line I hold, full stop. What wears me down is watching good machinists and assemblers absorb the culture shift from Pete's family shop to TASI's reporting cadence — quotas, comp structures, the sense that autonomy is being metered out from Michigan or wherever corporate sits this quarter. I don't resent TASI; the balance sheet is real and useful. But I notice the seam between theater execution and higher command every single day now, and I'm the one standing in it.
