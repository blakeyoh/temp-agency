# Pyromation, Inc. — context

Independent designer/manufacturer of industrial temperature sensing devices. Fort Wayne,
Indiana (5211 and 5227 Industrial Road). Founded 1962 by Dick Wilson and Bill Lewis as a
service/sales shop for industrial oven temperature controls; pivoted to thermocouple
fabrication in 1968. Pete Wilson became President and Owner in 1992 and ran the company for
thirty years. **Acquired by TASI Group in November 2022**; now an operating business inside
the TASI Measurement segment alongside AW-Lake, ONICON, Seametrics, and SignalFire Wireless
Telemetry. Wilson retired from the presidency in 2023–2024. The plant runs on a decentralized
model — Fort Wayne keeps its manufacturing agility, engineering identity, and brand, and gains
the parent's balance sheet.

## Scale
- Est. 120–150 employees: precision machining, sensor assembly, metrology
- 60+ years in operation; distribution into 45+ countries
- ISO 9001:2015, ISO 14001, NVLAP metrology accreditation (Lab Code 200502-0, NIST-traceable)
- Standard custom build-to-order in **4 working days**; express same-day, 24-hr, 72-hr
- Two-tier approval since 2022: Fort Wayne executive (Sawyer, Beckman) *and* TASI corporate (Norris)

## Products
Thermocouples (Types J/K/T/E/N base metal; R/S/B noble metal; MgO mineral-insulated sheaths;
-200 °C to 2,316 °C). RTDs (Pt100/Pt1000, 2/3/4-wire, wire-wound and thin-film; 3-A Sanitary
CIP/SIP designs). Thermowells and protection tubes (gun-drilled solid barstock and welded pipe;
316/304 stainless, Hastelloy, Inconel, Monel, Titanium). Transmitters (head- and DIN-rail mounted,
4–20 mA, HART, Bluetooth, ATEX/IECEx/CSA/SIL). In-house NVLAP calibration lab.
End markets: chemical/petrochemical, power generation, food & beverage (3-A), aerospace
(AMS 2750), biopharma. Go-to-market is hybrid: direct OEM design wins plus a distributor and
manufacturer's-rep network (e.g. FCX Performance).

## Known strengths
Speed is the moat — 4 days against 3–8 weeks from the tier-one conglomerates, which makes the
company indispensable for unplanned plant outages and MRO. That speed rests on unusual vertical
integration: they machine their own thermowells from barstock, cast their own patented connection
heads, and compact their own MgO cable on site, so scheduling and dimensional quality are theirs
to control. The NVLAP lab lets them serve audit-intensive customers who reject suppliers without
documented national calibration standards. TASI ownership added financial stability and access to
sister-company telemetry technology.

## Known weaknesses
The acquisition introduced real friction. Moving from Pete Wilson's multigenerational family
culture to TASI's corporate reporting structures has produced employee friction over production
quotas, compensation packages, and operational autonomy — a retention and morale risk concentrated
in the machining and assembly value streams. Commercially the company is insulated: a large share
of revenue moves through distributors and independent reps, so Pyromation often cannot see the
technical pain points of the plant engineers actually running its devices, which slows discovery
and product iteration. It remains a hardware firm, not a digital one — it integrates third-party
transmitters but writes no predictive-maintenance algorithms, cloud analytics, or asset-health
software of its own. Brand recognition is concentrated in North America; large international
capex projects require the parent to intervene.

## Opportunities
Bundle Pyromation sensor and thermowell assemblies with SignalFire's self-powered hazardous-area
wireless nodes to sell turnkey wireless temperature monitoring that skips plant-wide conduit and
signal cabling. Push 3-A sanitary RTDs into biopharma, API, and specialty food processing, where
electropolished CIP/SIP assemblies carry materially higher gross margins than base-metal
thermocouples. Serve the energy transition — clean hydrogen, high-pressure carbon capture, small
modular reactors — which needs exotic high-nickel thermowell geometries that in-house metallurgy
and gun-drilling can build. Digitize calibration traceability with laser-etched QR or embedded
RFID tied to cloud-stored NVLAP reports, so customers can stop tracking certificates on paper.

## Threats
Automation conglomerates (Emerson/Rosemount, Endress+Hauser, Siemens, Yokogawa) bundle valves,
flow meters, DCS, and sensors into single-vendor procurement packages, and EPC firms building
greenfield plants take the bundle — which marginalizes specialist sensor makers no matter how
good their custom fabrication is. Platinum wire, nickel and chromium, and refractory billets swing
in price and cannot always be passed through fixed distributor price schedules, so margins compress.
Northeast Indiana has a tight market for CNC programmers, gun-drill machinists, certified welders,
and metrology specialists against an aging workforce — that is a wage and a capacity problem at
once. Low-cost Asian manufacturers keep commoditizing standardized base-metal assemblies.

## Culture note
An engineering and shop-floor culture, sixty years deep, three years into being owned by someone
else. Leadership is grown from within — Sawyer started as a manufacturing engineer here in 2004,
Craghead has run marketing for twenty years, Moritz built the CAD/CAM backbone himself. Proof is
physical and documented: a wake-frequency calculation, a calibration certificate, a part that ships
in four days. Abstract initiatives without a documented return do not clear the new two-tier
approval gate. The open question everyone is living inside is how much of the old autonomy
survives the new reporting structure.
