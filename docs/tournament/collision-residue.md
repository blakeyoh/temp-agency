# Collision Residue

> A ledger of mechanisms that become visible because two entrants collide and that neither
> entrant already contains. Residue never changes the game that produced it, never enters the
> active field and is never smuggled into a winner as absorption.

## Why this exists

The Round of 32 generated several of its most interesting ideas accidentally inside panel
notes. The tournament preserved the winner and absorption ruling while leaving these third
ideas difficult to discover. Beginning with the Sweet 16, Collision Residue becomes a required
post-verdict artifact.

Collision Residue differs from the existing dispositions:

| Artifact | Question |
|---|---|
| ABSORBED | What part of the loser is required by the winner's existing thesis? |
| ORTHOGONAL | Which existing mechanisms should remain independent? |
| SUBSUMED | What was already present in the winner? |
| **COLLISION RESIDUE** | What new mechanism became visible only because these two met? |

The panel writes residue only after sealing its score, overall vote and absorption disposition.
This prevents the excitement of inventing a third idea from anchoring the game verdict.

## Round-of-32 seed discoveries

These ideas were surfaced by the panels but were not entrants in the original field.

### CR-R32-G9 · Disagreement sourced from differing evidence

**Collision:** Lens Transformers × Stochastic Persona Fracture.

**Source:** Ecologist, Game 9.

**Mechanism:** Give two isolated readers differently transformed evidence rather than one
shared text plus an instruction to disagree. Each reader receives a different half, redaction
or transformation of the artifact; disagreement arises from incompatible evidence states.

**Why neither parent contains it:** Lens Transformers changes what one persona sees.
Persona Fracture creates instructed conflict inside one profile. This residue distributes
different evidence across independent actors and allows the disagreement to emerge from the
information boundary.

**Non-adjacency axis:** actor and mechanism.

**Status:** Candidate for a future field. Also recorded as a separate-build finding in
`parallel-track.md`.

### CR-R32-G10 · Priced defection

**Collision:** Make the Problem Strange First × Committee of Strangers.

**Source:** Ecologist, Game 10.

**Mechanism:** A persona may defect from a coalition only by naming a falsifiable price: the
specific evidence, concession, resource or future observation that would buy its support.
The price persists and can be checked later.

**Why neither parent contains it:** Make the Problem Strange First transforms a brief.
Committee of Strangers requires a defection to state its price but supplies no durable or
resolvable meaning for price. The residue turns rhetoric into a dated commitment that can
become state.

**Non-adjacency axis:** failure mode and timescale.

**Status:** Candidate for a future field. Also recorded as a separate-build finding in
`parallel-track.md`.

### CR-R32-G12 · Forced-versus-chosen claims

**Collision:** Homogeneity Auditor × Telegram Constraint.

**Source:** the unresolved correct-answer objection in Game 12; proposed after the Blind
Auditor amendment and approved by the commissioner, untested.

**Mechanism:** The blind median reconstruction labels each claim **forced** when the problem's
facts or logic require it and **chosen** when it reflects a framing, structure, example or
interpretive move. The gate rejects overlap only on chosen claims.

**Why neither parent contains it:** The original Auditor rejects overlap without classifying
why the overlap exists. The Telegram prices expression without distinguishing mandatory
content from discretionary framing. This residue introduces a claim-level causal type that
protects correct obviousness while still rejecting imaginative default.

**Non-adjacency axis:** mechanism and failure mode.

**Status:** Commissioner-approved amendment component for the Blind Auditor; still retained
here because the collision produced a general-purpose distinction that may outlive that entrant.

## Sweet 16 entry standard

Each game may add zero or more panel proposals. A candidate is recorded only when it passes:

1. **Neither-parent test:** removing either parent from the history makes the idea unlikely to
   appear in this form.
2. **Non-adjacency test:** it differs from both parents on a named axis among mechanism,
   actor, failure mode or timescale.
3. **One-sentence test:** the mechanism can be stated without “and also.”
4. **No retroactive force:** it cannot change the game result, repair a parent or enter the
   current bracket.

Use this template:

```markdown
### CR-S16-G#-PANEL · Name

**Collision:** A × B.
**Source:** panel and game.
**Mechanism:** One sentence.
**Why neither parent contains it:** ...
**Non-adjacency axis:** mechanism | actor | failure mode | timescale.
**Characteristic failure:** ...
**Status:** Candidate | Duplicate | Subsumed by prior residue.
```

At the commissioner gate, convergent residue proposals are consolidated only when they make
the same claim. Distinct proposals remain distinct; cleanliness is not a reason to merge them.
