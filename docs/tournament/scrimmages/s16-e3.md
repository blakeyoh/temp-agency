**Improvised routing record:** The roster index describes the Nuclear Reactor Operator as
appropriate for irreversible operations, bounded-error control, live migrations, and

**Pre-job brief:** Treat a first approach as a controlled evolution. Establish the current
state from observable context, identify operating limits such as attention and consent,
credit more than one barrier against an awkward approach, execute one short opening, verify
the response, and stop cleanly when the indication is unfavorable. Ten distinct openings
derived from that spine:

1. **Establish current state:** “Hi, I’m [name]. What brought you to this conference?”
2. **Check the useful indication:** “Which session or conversation has been most useful to you so far?”
3. **Name the desired condition:** “What problem are you hoping to make progress on while you’re here?”
4. **Check operating history:** “Is this your first time here, or do you already know the conference’s rhythm?”
5. **Use an observed signal only:** “I noticed you were looking at the program. What are you deciding between?”
6. **Test transfer to the home system:** “What’s one idea from today you’d like to take back to your team?”
7. **Identify the current work state:** “What kind of work occupies most of your time right now?”
8. **Invite an independent indication:** “I’m comparing notes across the field. What part of this work do you see differently?”
9. **Set a bounded evolution:** “Would you be open to a two-minute exchange? I can share what brought me here, and you can share yours.”
10. **Use a timed, reversible handoff:** “Before the next session starts, may I introduce myself and ask what you’re here to learn?”

For every item, the expected response is a willing answer or a reciprocal question. A short
answer, turned body, no eye contact, or other unfavorable indication is a hold point, not a
problem to overcome. Recover with “Thanks, enjoy the conference,” and terminate the
evolution. The credited barriers are a plain self-introduction, one answerable question,
an explicit or implicit easy exit, and response verification before any follow-up.

### LENS PASS — Behavioral Psychologist

**Bounded objection:** The lead's control-room frame creates a useful stop rule, but it risks
treating a badge glance, program glance, or body-language cue as a trustworthy indication.
Those cues are context-dependent, and several lead questions ask for reflective answers that
may impose more friction than a stranger wants in the first seconds. The conference setting
also supplies defaults and social pressure, so a script can become an intervention even when
it is presented as neutral.

Keep the ten lead-derived openings, but apply three bounded checks. Use an observed cue only
when it is real and relevant, prefer one short question over a forced mini-interview, and
make the exit genuinely easy. Judge an opener by observed continuation, such as whether the
person answers and asks back, rather than by a claim that people say they would like it. This
objection limits misuse of the lead's list; it does not replace its state-limits-barriers-
verification-recovery spine with a behavioral plan.
