The 99th
Idea Bracket

Thirty-two mechanisms for forcing randomness — and therefore creativity — into a model trained toward consistency. Cross-pollinated into four brackets so every opening game is a cross-theory argument, judged by three independent panels blind to seed, region and authorship, scored comparatively against fixed reference points, and halted after every round for the commissioner to rule.

A creative workshop asks a room for 100 ways to protect a home. The first ten are a fence, a dog, a camera. The last ten are a moat and a fleet of UFOs. The value was never the moat — it is that reaching idea #99 requires a different generative process than reaching idea #3, and temp-agency currently stops at about #12.

where this stands

Round of 32, three brackets judged

Brackets 1 and 2 are complete and ruled. Bracket 3 (Moat) has been judged by all three panels and is awaiting commissioner rulings — its results below are panel verdicts, not final placements. Bracket 4 has a packet written and has not been dispatched. Every decision below is recorded in docs/tournament/commissioner-rulings.md, which is the source of truth; overrides are printed as overrides and never folded into a panel's verdict.

The tournament eliminated itself

Bracket as a Primitive — the mechanism generating this entire document — lost 19–7 in round one, unanimously, to judges blind to what they were evaluating.

Skeptic: “It is the entrant that most flatters the process now evaluating it, and an idea that rewards its own judging mechanism earns harder questions, not softer ones… 32 entrants with a refusal-by-default absorption rule means 24 of them contribute nothing at all.”
Ecologist: “Its cost per use — 31 matchups against five axes with two predicates each — is its own Limits to Growth loop whose limiting condition is judge attention, and judge attention does not scale into year three.”

The second prediction was confirmed within the hour: this session paused for usage limits after two of four brackets. The Skeptic's charge remains open and unresolved — it lands on a live design decision, not on any entrant.

how a game is decided

Comparative scoring, three blind panels

Run 1 was judged by one model assigning absolute 1–10 scores with mandatory absorption. It produced compressed, unfalsifiable numbers and a tidy narrative. Run 2 fixes all three.

AxisThe question it asksRequired predicate — no predicate, no vote
DistanceHow far from the median AI answer does this push the output?“Name a specific output this forbids.”
MechanismDoes something actually execute, or is this exhortation?“Name the file that runs.”
IrreducibilityCould the base model already do this if simply asked nicely?“A base model given only the prompt ___ reproduces about ___% of this.”
CompoundingDoes it get weirder with use, or does the novelty wear off?“State what use #10 produces that use #1 does not.”
Generative failureWhen it breaks, does it produce a different kind of insight?“Describe the exact failure output.”

Comparative only

No standalone scores. Per axis a panel votes significantly better (3), slightly better (1), or Tie. Three panels sum to a range of −9…+9 per axis, −45…+45 per game.

A fixed ruler

Each axis pins named reference points from floor to ceiling. Judges decide which references an idea sits between — a difficulty table, not an impression.

Blind panels

Builder (Mechanism bias), Skeptic (Irreducibility), Ecologist (Compounding). Blind to seed, region, authorship, and each other. A/B position flipped on 10 of 16 games from seed 99.

Absorption refuses by default

Three tests, all must pass: same-thesis, deletion (worse or merely smaller?), one-sentence. Dispositions are ABSORBED / ORTHOGONAL / SUBSUMED — and orthogonal is a finding.
