## Candidate 1 — Enforced withholding *(Group 3: A1, C8, M1)*

**Sourced from:** `docs/tournament/scrimmages/s16-a1.md`, `s16-c8.md`, `s16-m1.md` —
three independently-run scrimmages, each testing an unrelated mechanism, converged on the
identical failure without seeing each other's work.

**Proposed rule text:**
> A mechanism whose contract requires generation from a transformed, masked, or withheld
> view is enacted only when the generating pass runs in a separate context that receives the
> transformed artifact and nothing else; a same-context run records that operation as NOT RUN.

**Creativity rationale:** withholding *is* the generative process — when the raw brief
stays visible, output regresses to the median (A1's ten generic openers, C8's conventional
restored list, M1's un-blind auditor). This is not audit plumbing; it is the difference
between the mechanism existing and being theater.

- **Commissioner disposition:** PENDING

## Candidate 2 — Counterfactual-draw test *(Group 2: E1)*

**Sourced from:** `docs/tournament/scrimmages/s16-e1.md`, sharpened beyond its own docket
proposal.

**Proposed rule text:**
> An official E1 run must include one counterfactual replay: re-draw at least one opener's
> indices under a second stamped seed and show materially different prose for that opener.
> If the changed draw leaves the prose materially unchanged, the run records decorative
> randomness as a contract failure.

**Creativity rationale:** E1 ("The Entropy Well") is the tournament's foundational
randomness primitive — E6's card draw and A6's mutation seed both assume a seed actually
moves the output. Without this test, the model can write its preferred opener under any
label and the seed becomes theater.

- **Commissioner disposition:** PENDING

## Candidate 3 — Deletion test for foreign-frame mechanisms *(Group 1: E2, E3)*

**Sourced from:** E2's own docket (`s16-e2.md`), generalized because E3 (`s16-e3.md`)
exhibits the identical failure with no proposed cure.

**Proposed rule text:**
> Where an entrant's contract claims a foreign frame — a foraged artifact, a wrong-expert
> derivation, or similar — shapes the output, the enforcer must run a per-item deletion test:
> if removing the foreign frame leaves an item's substance unchanged, the item is rejected and
> regenerated under the frame. A pass requires at least three surviving frame-dependent items.

**Creativity rationale:** the reject-and-retry loop forces the foreign object to actually
deform the ideas rather than decorate them — the different generative process the north star
requires, not an audit trail.

- **Commissioner disposition:** PENDING

## Candidate 5 — Killed-hybrid ledger *(Group 3: E4)*

**Sourced from:** E4's own docket (`s16-e4.md`).

**Proposed rule text:**
> Every E4 death is appended to a killed-hybrid ledger keyed by parent pair, seed, exact
> mutation, score, and cause of death; `bin/breed` must read the ledger and refuse to re-emit

**Creativity rationale:** persisted death is selection pressure — it walls off proven-sterile
regions so each generation must search untried recombinations, compounding across runs

## Candidate 7 — Draw upstream of curation *(Group 4: E6)*

**Sourced from:** synthesized from E6's disclosure (`s16-e6.md`), sharper than its own
count-check proposal.

**Proposed rule text:**
> An official E6 run must count the real `knowledge/*/positions.md` files at dispatch, and
> the seeded draw must select both the source packs and the card from that full counted
> corpus; the operator may not pre-select which packs feed distillation, and a missing
> requested pack fails the draw closed.

**Creativity rationale:** E6's scrimmage produced genuinely strange lines ("labor to
spare") but only within a 2-persona space the operator curated in advance — pushing the
randomness upstream of curation widens the tail the draw can actually reach.

## Selection notes (for reference, not a ruling)

Candidates 1 and 2 were flagged as the strongest by cross-group synthesis: candidate 1 on
independent-convergence grounds (the same standard the tournament already uses for absorption
rulings — see Ruling 9), candidate 2 because E1 is load-bearing infrastructure other entrants
depend on. Candidates 4 and 7 were rated "moderate" fit by their own analysis groups.
Candidates 3, 5, and 6 are each scoped to a single entrant. None of this ranking binds the
ruling — it is the reasoning trail, not a recommendation to follow mechanically.

## Appendix: full analysis reports

### Group 1 (E3, M5, E2, A3)

**Enactment gaps:**
- **E3** — The inversion ran but only restructured the answer's spine; all ten surface
  openers stayed conventional conference questions under control-room labels, a "thin
  costume" the record itself names (`s16-e3.md:149-151, 174`).
- **M5** — Nothing ran: no corpus, embeddings, clusters, threshold, or gate exist, so the
  record is a candidate list plus a hand-written prediction of what a pipeline might find
  (`s16-m5.md:102-107`).
- **E2** — The random fetch genuinely ran, but the load-bearing check was operator
  self-review with no rejection authority, and 7 of 10 items were untouched by the artifact
  (`s16-e2.md:236, 249`).
- **A3** — The one real tool measured repo git history, irrelevant to the brief, while the
  persona-defining tools (`bin/claims` etc.) remain model judgments in executable costume
  (`s16-a3.md:118-122`).

Docket items rated polish, not creativity-expanding: E3's routing-record proposal
(`s16-e3.md:161` — "makes the... inversion auditable," by its own words) and A3's
costumed-tool removal (`s16-a3.md:129`). M5 proposed nothing, correctly — its gap is unbuilt
machinery, not a rule defect.

### Group 2 (E5, E1, A5, A6)

**Enactment gaps:**
- **E5** — Lexicon, checker, and rejection loop are all the same operator judgment that
  produced the draft; leak detection and the reject decision share one source (`s16-e5.md:138`).
- **E1** — The PRNG genuinely ran, but the index-to-prose mapping is unchecked — no
  independent confirmation the prose really matches the drawn indices (`s16-e1.md:175,182`).
- **A5** — The "hostile environment" was a self-imposed instruction inside the answering
  model's own context; the cutoff was inspectable and could have been ignored, and items 1–4
  were untouched, competent, and modal (`s16-a5.md:80,98-99`).
- **A6** — All three degradation errors ran, but only through per-run manual prompting;
  preservation depends on operator discipline, not a rejection mechanism (`s16-a6.md:129`).

Docket items rated polish or counter-productive: E5's typed log boundary is necessary
plumbing but pure auditability. A5's run manifest is weakest — it hardens a truncation
harness whose own record proves the degradation changed nothing creatively.

### Group 3 (A1, C8, E4, M1)

**Enactment gaps:**
- **A1** — The word-length transform ran deterministically, but no context boundary
  existed — the responder sat in the same context as the raw brief (`s16-a1.md:117, 127`).
- **C8** — Masking was manual substitution inside a context that still contained the
  original nouns; its own defect test proved untouched words (`open`, `List`, `distinct`)
  carry the priors the mask claims to remove (`s16-c8.md:110, 121, 88-94`).
- **E4** — The crossover splice was real, but seed 271828 selected nothing and the kill
  verdict persists nowhere — no future breeding run can read this child's cause of death
  (`s16-e4.md:196-200, 241`).
- **M1** — Both passes ran in one continuous Codex session, so the "blind" auditor was
  never blind and median independence cannot be established (`s16-m1.md:159-165, 181`).

Docket items rated polish or counter-productive: C8's claim-narrowing (`s16-c8.md:128`)
makes the docs honest but generates nothing new. M1's FORCED/AMBIGUOUS rule (`s16-m1.md:189`)
makes the gate safer by *reducing* rejection pressure — protective, not expansive.

### Group 4 (M3, A2, C5, E6)

**Enactment gaps:**
- **M3** — With no ledger, writer, comparator, or gate, and an empty history, every
  comparison is vacuously "extension" — the rejection gate has never fired (`s16-m3.md:136`).
- **A2** — No training corpus, checkpoint, or harness exists; the only output was a
  standalone large-model solve bearing the forbidden signature (`s16-a2.md:108`). Its record
  correctly proposes no amendment — too far from runnable to warrant one.
- **C5** — The notation-first order rests on operator say-so; no external trace can rule
  out prose drafted first and transcribed into the checklist (`s16-c5.md:223,228`).
- **E6** — The draw was real but ran over a deck the operator hand-built from 2 of 16
  available files — randomness only chose among options the operator had already tamed
  (`s16-e6.md:33-37,101`).

Docket items rated polish: C5's registry/validator proposal (`s16-c5.md:239`) and E6's bare
count-check improve auditability only, not creative reach.
